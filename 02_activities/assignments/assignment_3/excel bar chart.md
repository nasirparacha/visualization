What software did you use to create your data visualization?
I used Microsoft Excel to create the bar chart. Excel is widely accessible and provides a simple interface for quickly summarizing and visualizing categorical data like air conditioning types.

Who is your intended audience?
The intended audience includes Toronto tenants, housing researchers, and municipal planners. These groups can use the visualization to understand the availability of air conditioning across rental buildings in the city.

What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization?
The bar chart illustrates the frequency of different air conditioning systems (e.g., none, central, individual units) in apartment buildings. The message is that a significant number of buildings may lack adequate cooling, which has implications for comfort, equity, and climate resilience.

What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots?
Design considerations included clear labels, sorted bars, and contrasting colors to improve readability. I applied these by labeling the horizontal axis with the count of buildings, using a bold title, and ordering bars from highest to lowest to emphasize the most common types.

How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization?
To ensure reproducibility, I organized the dataset into a separate sheet, used pivot table to generate counts, and documented each step within the Excel file. While Excel is not as reproducible as code-based tools, following a clear workflow with formulas helps others replicate or update the chart if new data is provided.

How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?
I used high-contrast colors, large fonts, and data labels on each bar to make the chart readable for users with visual impairments. I also included a text summary beneath the chart for screen readers or users who may not interpret visuals easily.

Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?
Renters, housing advocates, and city officials may be impacted by this visualization, as it highlights potential gaps in climate preparedness. It may also influence building policy by drawing attention to buildings lacking any form of air conditioning.

How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization?
I selected only the AIR_CONDITIONING_TYPE column because it directly aligned with the research question. I excluded other columns such as address, postal code, or building owner, as they were not necessary for analyzing overall AC availability.

What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?
Behind the scenes, I had to clean the data, ensure consistent naming in the air conditioning type field, extract unique categories, and manually structure the data for counting and charting. This preparation work, though not visible in the final chart, was essential for accuracy and clarity.
